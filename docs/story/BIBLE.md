## 1. Logline, premise, tone

### Logline
*In a kingdom where every promise is written in a holy book and every broken one comes back as a
monster, a nameless sellsword discovers that the church keeping the record has been rewriting it —
and that the oldest promise of his life, made on a rooftop with an orphan girl, is the only true
entry left.*

### One-paragraph premise
The Kingdom of **Aldmere** runs on oaths. Fealty, marriage, debt, treaty and trade are sworn through
**Oathcraft** and written into **the Ledger**, the church-bureaucracy whose clerics draw holy
power from promises kept. A promise broken does not simply vanish: its weight curdles into a
**Hollow**, a hungry shade that hunts the breaker. Twelve years ago King Edric died of a "Hollow
fever" and his ten-year-old daughter vanished; his brother **Lucan** has ruled as Regent ever since,
hard and grey and slowly dying. **Bren Holloway**, a foundling the Ledger never bothered to write
down, makes his living escorting caravans through Hollow country with a free company called
Brask's Lanterns. When a contract reunites him with **Hesper Vane** — the girl he grew up with in a
Ledger orphanage, now a rising novice of the church — a locked box of old records sets the Regent's
soldiers, the church's inquisitors and the kingdom's forgotten poor on a collision course. What is
written in that box will crown a queen, break a friendship, and expose the oldest lie in the realm:
that only the Ledger can make a promise real.

### What the story is about (themes)
- **The cost of promises.** Every oath in this world has weight. Characters are defined by the
  promises they keep, the ones they break, and the ones they break *for the right reasons*.
- **Who keeps the record of truth.** The Ledger decides what is true by deciding what is written.
  Names can be struck. Debts can be moved. History can be filed in a drawer. Our heroes have only
  their memories and each other as witnesses.
- **Class.** About a third of Aldmere is *unrecorded*: too poor to pay the Naming fee, they cannot
  own land, swear recorded oaths or testify. They are also, secretly, where the realm's unpaid
  debts are dumped.
- **Loyalty vs. conscience.** Every major character is loyal to something — a friend, a king, a
  church, a village, a dead brother — and each is forced to choose between that loyalty and what
  they know is right.
- **Moral greyness.** The Regent is a tyrant who is dying of love. The Ashguard burn villages to save
  provinces. The Ledger's lie really did end an age of chaos. The hero's best friend is right to
  want to change the system from inside. Nobody is a cartoon.

### Tone
- **Grounded political fantasy with a supernatural engine.** Think muddy roads, tithe barns,
  courtrooms and chapels; the uncanny is rare, specific and frightening.
- **Warm at the campfire, cold at court.** The company banter is genuinely funny; the politics are
  genuinely dangerous. Humour comes from character, never from winking at the audience.
- **Earned emotion.** We do not tell the player to be sad. We let them spend time with people, and
  then we take something away.
- **Readable, speakable, quotable.** Lines are written to be voiced. The best lines are short.

### Reference touchstones (for feel, not for copying)
The political chessboard and church conspiracy of classic tactics RPGs; the found-family company of
mercenary sagas; the moral weight of folk tales about bargains and debts; the paperwork menace of a
bureaucracy that believes in itself. **We copy no plot, names, places or characters from any
existing game.**

## 2. The world: history and timeline

Years are counted **AL — "after the Ledger"** — from the founding of the Archive by Saint Verity.
The present day of Chapter 1 is **634 AL**, late autumn.

### Deep history
- **The Age of Broken Words (before 0 AL).** The kingdoms of the old continent swore and broke
  promises freely. Hollows multiplied until whole provinces were "eaten" — forests of grey ash
  where nothing grew. Folk tales of this age give us the beast-headed Hollows still seen today.
- **0 AL — The First Witness.** **Verity of the Mere**, a lakeside scribe, began writing down every
  oath sworn in her valley and binding them before witnesses. Kept oaths shone; broken ones could
  be found and paid. Her followers became **the Ledger**. Whether she discovered that writing an
  oath *strengthens* it, or merely convinced people it did, is the oldest question in the world.
- **3 AL — The Crown Oath.** The warlord **Aldmer** swore the first Crown Oath in Verity's book
  ("I will keep the realm, and the realm will keep me") and became the first King of Aldmere.
  Every monarch since has sworn it at coronation and been written into the Ledger.
- **3–300 AL — The Long Peace.** The Ledger spreads. Hollows become rare. The church grows into a
  bureaucracy: chanceries in every province, scribes in every village.
- **412 AL — The Naming Fee.** Archscribe Dolenta introduces a fee for writing a newborn's
  **Naming** into the Ledger "to pay for ink and vellum". Within two generations a third of the
  population is unrecorded. This is the root of the class divide.
- **c. 450 AL — The First Remittance.** (Secret. Known only to Archscribes and the Black Folio.)
  An Archscribe discovers that a recorded oath can be **struck** from the book, sparing its breaker
  — and that the struck debt must fall somewhere: on the nearest *unrecorded* souls. The Ledger
  begins selling **Strikings** to the powerful, quietly, and calls them "remissions".
- **588 AL — The Treaty of Seven Rivers** between Aldmere and the Velmont Concord.
- **594 AL — The Ashen Reach.** King Osric (Edric and Lucan's father) breaks the Treaty to seize the
  river trade. The Ledger strikes his treaty oath; its enormous debt is remitted onto the
  unrecorded peasantry of the eastern provinces. Within a year the east is a Hollow blight.
  Officially, *Velmont* broke the treaty and the blight is their curse. The **Ashguard** are founded
  to burn ("ash") Hollow-stricken villages and hold the blight's border.
- **609 AL — King Edric I crowned.** Edric "the Fair", a scholarly, stubborn king.
- **612 AL — Princess Aurelie Isolde Valcourt born.** Queen Isolde dies of a fever in 615.
- **621 AL — Edric discovers the Remittance.** Reading his father's sealed papers, Edric learns what
  the Ledger did in 594. He begins quietly gathering evidence to break the Ledger's power.
- **622 AL — The Night of the Striking.** Archscribe **Clement** strikes Edric's own Crown Oath from
  the Ledger. Thirteen years of a king's promises — to a whole realm — curdle at once. Edric dies
  within the hour, "of a Hollow fever". Hollows tear through the palace quarter of Aurenne. Nurse
  **Maud Ellery** flees with the princess; Maud is killed on the road; the child is found fevered
  by young Auditor **Ignace Morrow**, who brings her to the remote Ledger foundling house at
  **Millbrook** and, on Clement's order, strikes her Naming. She is renamed **Hesper Vane** and
  recovers with almost no memory. At the same hour, Edric's brother **Lucan**, at his bedside,
  swears a deathbed oath to "find Aurelie and keep her safe, and see her crowned". The Ledger
  proclaims Lucan Regent "until the heir is found".
- **622 AL — Bren meets Hesper.** Bren, eleven, a foundling at Millbrook since infancy, is the boy
  holding the lamp at the door the night "Hesper" is carried in.
- **624 AL — The rooftop oath.** Bren (13) and Hesper (12), denied a Witness because orphans cannot
  pay, swear an unwitnessed oath on the foundling house roof and split a penny between them.
- **626 AL** — Hesper (14) is taken into the Ledger novitiate at Aurenne, sponsored by Morrow.
  Bren (15) is sold into indenture to the free company Brask's Lanterns.
- **627–633 AL — The Regency hardens.** Lucan's deathbed oath goes unkept; he sickens with ash in
  his veins. He becomes harsher, more desperate. The Ashguard are turned on Hollow-struck
  villages across the Lowmarch.
- **632 AL — Sallowmere is ashed** by Captain Ilka Stroud's Ashguard. Tamsin Reed survives.
- **634 AL — Present day.** Chapter 1 begins.

### The present political moment (634 AL)
- The Regent is visibly dying and everyone knows it. Who rules after him?
- The Ledger is richer and more confident than ever. Archscribe Clement is beloved.
- Hollows are increasing in the Lowmarch and along the Ashen Reach. Nobody official will say why.
- The unrecorded are restless. In the fens, people have begun swearing **hedge oaths** to each other
  in secret — a heresy. The church calls them "the Unwritten".
- The Duke of the Westmarch has begun asking, loudly, whether a Regent who cannot find the heir in
  twelve years should still be Regent.

## 3. Oathcraft, the Ledger and the Hollows — the rules

**Writers: these rules are load-bearing. Break them only on purpose, on screen, with consequences.**
Each rule is tagged **[PUBLIC]** (what ordinary people believe), **[TRUE]** (how it actually works)
or **[SECRET]** (true, and actively hidden by the Ledger). The story is the slow movement of rules
from SECRET to PUBLIC.

### 3.1 Oaths
1. **[PUBLIC] An oath is a promise sworn with intent before a Witness.** The Ledger teaches that
   only an ordained Ledger Witness can bind an oath, and only by writing it in a Ledger book with
   sealing ink while both parties speak the words.
2. **[TRUE] An oath binds when it is sworn with true intent before at least one witness who
   accepts the role.** The witness may be anyone — including the other party. Writing is not
   necessary. A Ledger book merely *records* the oath (and, see 3.4, gives the Ledger power over
   it). Oaths sworn without the Ledger are called **hedge oaths**.
3. **[TRUE] Weight.** Every oath has a weight (its *surety*): roughly, how much was staked and how
   many people rely on it. A child's promise to a friend is light. A marriage is heavier. A lord's
   fealty, heavier. A king's Crown Oath — sworn to a whole realm — is the heaviest oath there is.
4. **[TRUE] Keeping an oath gives off grace.** A kept oath strengthens the one who keeps it — subtly
   (steadier hands, clearer head) for ordinary people, overtly for those trained to draw on it.
5. **[TRUE] The forfeit.** An oath that *names its own forfeit* ("…or may I lose my right hand")
   caps its debt at that forfeit. This old hedge-witness technique is how small, safe oaths can be
   sworn lightly. The Ledger forbids named forfeits ("the Witness decides the price"), which is why
   Ledger oaths can spawn Hollows of any size.
6. **The words.** A Ledger oath ends "**Witnessed, and kept.**" A hedge oath has no fixed form; the
   Lanternbearer tradition uses "**I witness you.**" — answered "**And I you.**"

### 3.2 Hollows
1. **[PUBLIC] When a sworn oath is broken, the breaker's debt manifests as a Hollow** — a hungry
   shade that hunts the oathbreaker. Hollows are the Witness's judgement.
2. **[TRUE] Size follows weight.** A light oath broken makes a small Hollow (a flicker, a cold
   dog-shape that whines at the window). A heavy oath makes a beast. A Crown Oath breaks a city.
3. **[TRUE] Timing.** A Hollow gathers over hours to years — faster for heavier oaths and in fog,
   dark, and wet ground. Hollows are strongest at night and in marshland.
4. **[TRUE] Hunger.** A Hollow seeks its debtor. It will harm anyone between it and them. It cannot
   cross running water easily (it must "ford", slowly) and hates *witnesslight* (3.5).
5. **[TRUE] Forms.** Hollows take shape from fear and memory. Fresh, small ones are formless
   shadows or spectral hounds. Older ones *settle* into folk-tale shapes — wolf-headed hunters, boar-
   headed brutes, horned bulls, little grasping "tithelings". A Hollow that finds a corpse can wear
   it: these are the **Restless** (skeletons, ghouls). A Hollow born of a noble's debt sometimes
   wears a noble face and feeds on grace: a **Gaunt**.
6. **[TRUE] Ending a Hollow.** Destroying a Hollow disperses it for a while (hours to weeks). It
   ends for good only if the debt is *paid* — the oath kept late, the wronged party compensated, or
   the debtor's own grace spent in a deliberate act of atonement. When a debtor dies unpaid, the
   Hollow lingers where they died: haunted graveyards are full of old debts.
7. **[TRUE] Blight.** When enough unpaid debt pools in one place, the land itself goes Hollow:
   grey ash soil, dead trees, colourless sky, Hollows rising nightly. The Ashen Reach is blight.

### 3.3 Naming
1. **[PUBLIC] A Naming** is the oath sworn on a newborn's behalf by parents before a Ledger Witness,
   recording the child as a soul of Aldmere. Named people can own land, inherit, swear recorded
   oaths, testify and hold office. Naming costs a fee — two silver marks in the Lowmarch, more in
   the Crownlands.
2. **[PUBLIC] The unrecorded** — people without a Naming — are about a third of the realm: fen
   folk, foundlings, the rural poor, the children of the unrecorded. Many carry a "place-name" in
   lieu of a surname: Tenpenny, Sallowmere, **Holloway** (the name Ledger houses give foundlings —
   "the empty road").
3. **[SECRET] A Naming can be struck.** The struck person still lives and remembers who they are,
   but legally — and, as far as Oathcraft is concerned — they become unrecorded. Oaths made *to*
   them as their old self can no longer be kept (see Lucan, §7).

### 3.4 The Ledger's power over recorded oaths
1. **[PUBLIC] Remission.** The Ledger can, for grave cause and a donation, "remit" a broken oath —
   the Witness forgives the debt and the Hollow fades.
2. **[SECRET] Striking and the Remittance.** The Archscribe, or an Auditor carrying a warrant
   sealed by the Archscribe, can **strike** a recorded oath. A struck oath can no longer be broken,
   so its breaker is spared — but its weight does not vanish. It falls on the nearest **unrecorded**
   souls, by kinship of place (the village the oath was sworn for, the tenants of the lord who swore
   it). This is the **Remittance**. It is why the unrecorded poor of the Lowmarch suffer Hollows for
   oaths they never swore. The Ledger has sold Strikings to nobles for 180 years.
3. **[SECRET] Striking can kill.** Striking an oath *while it is being kept* tears its grace out of
   the keeper. Strike a light oath and the keeper feels faint. Strike a king's Crown Oath and the
   king dies. This is how Edric was murdered.
4. **[SECRET] Struck entries are not destroyed.** They are copied into the **Black Folio**, kept in
   the deepest vault of the Archive. The Black Folio is a complete history of the Remittance, and
   the most dangerous object in Aldmere.
5. **[TRUE] Hedge oaths cannot be struck.** They are not in the book. Their debts can only ever fall
   on the one who breaks them — which is why the Ledger fears them more than any army.
6. **[TRUE] The unrecorded cannot be struck**, because there is nothing written to strike. A
   foundling like Bren is immune to the Ledger's deepest weapon — but he is also exactly the kind of
   person remitted debts fall on.

### 3.5 Witnesslight and the lantern
**[TRUE]** Fire lit in the presence of a kept oath (or by a person keeping one) burns with a faint
clean steadiness — *witnesslight* — that Hollows hate. The Ledger sells "kept oil" for its lanterns
(oil blessed by Witnesses keeping their vows). The old hedge witnesses, the **Lanternbearers**,
carried lanterns to crossroads oath-swearings for the same reason. Odo Brask's lantern is one of
the last; Bren inherits it.

### 3.6 Holy magic, and other magic
- **Clerics** draw *grace* from oaths — their own kept vows and the surety of the faithful around
  them. **[PUBLIC]** The Ledger teaches grace flows only through the Ledger. **[TRUE]** Grace comes
  from kept promises anywhere; a cleric who leaves the church keeps her power as long as she keeps
  her word. (Hesper discovers this in Chapter 1.)
- **Arcane magic** (wizards, chronomancers, hexers, invokers) draws on **the Breath** — the
  elemental currents of the world. In Aldmere it is licensed by the Ledger; the great school is
  the Collegium of Saltreach. In the north, rune-casters carve the Breath into marks.
- **Oathbreakers** (the secret job) fight by *spending their own debt* — drawing on the Hollow
  that hunts them. Every blow costs them; every blow feeds the thing that will one day eat them.
- **Auditors** (the Ledger inquisition) are trained to read, seal and — with a warrant — strike.

### 3.7 Battle Oaths (gameplay ↔ fiction)
DESIGN §3.11's pre-battle Oaths are **hedge oaths with named forfeits**, witnessed by the company
— in Chapter 1, by Bren with Odo's lantern. Because each names its forfeit (Doubt: a loss of nerve
and Brave), breaking one can never spawn a true Hollow; keeping one gives off grace (the bonus).
- **Before the mid-chapter turn** (c1, before Tenpenny) the company only knows *Ledger* oaths;
  the Oath menu is locked.
- **At Tenpenny (c1.s16)** Eira explains hedge oaths, Bren lights Odo's lantern and the company
  swears to one another: they become **the Oathbound**. The Oath system unlocks with Bren as
  Witness. Story oaths unlock across the saga (see STORY_JOBS.md for the Lanternbearer's role).
- **In the fiction this is heresy.** Every battle oath the player swears is an act of rebellion
  against the Ledger, and the Ledger knows it.

### 3.8 Limits writers must respect
- Oaths do **not** grant wishes. They grant *grace* (luck, steadiness, holy power to the trained)
  and inflict *debt* (Hollows). That's all.
- Nobody can read minds through Oathcraft. An Auditor can tell if someone **is bound** to an oath
  by touch and long practice, not what the oath says — unless it is written down.
- A Hollow cannot be "aimed" at someone innocent — except through the Remittance, which aims only
  at the unrecorded by kinship of place. (The Ledger has never been able to direct it more
  precisely. This matters in Chapter 4.)
- Striking requires the **written entry** and the Archscribe's seal (in person or on a warrant).
- Hedge oaths are exactly as strong as Ledger oaths of the same weight. No more, no less.
- The dead do not come back. Restless wear corpses; they are not the person.

## 4. Religion, culture, economy, daily life

### 4.1 The faith of the Witness
- **The Witness** is the god of the Ledger: the one who sees every promise. It has no face and no
  statues; its symbol is **an open book with a single line drawn across both pages**.
- **Saint Verity** is the First Witness, depicted as a woman with ink-stained hands holding a
  lamp. Foundling houses, chapels and the Archive bear her name.
- **The Canon of Verity** is the scripture: aphorisms, oath-forms and parables. Characters quote it.
  Examples: *"What is written is kept."* · *"A word unwitnessed is wind."* · *"The book remembers
  what the heart forgets."* · *"Pay what you owe, and walk in the light."*
- **Common blessings and curses:** "Witness keep you." (goodbye) · "Witnessed." (agreed) · "Ink
  and ashes!" (surprise/anger) · "Hollow take it." (curse) · "Witness blind me." (strong oath) ·
  "On my Naming." (I swear it — used only by the Named; the unrecorded say "on my breath").
- **Holy days:** *Inkfast* (midwinter: debts are tallied), *Lampnight* (spring: lanterns lit for
  the dead), *Sealing Day* (midsummer: weddings and contracts).

### 4.2 Ledger ranks (lowest to highest)
| Rank | Role | Address |
|---|---|---|
| Novice | student, copyist | "Sister/Brother" |
| Scribe | ordained cleric, healer, record-keeper | "Sister/Brother" |
| Witness | may bind oaths | "Witness <name>" |
| Notary | administers a district's books, tithes, fees | "Notary" |
| Auditor | inquisitor; investigates breaches; may strike under warrant | "Auditor" |
| High Auditor | commands Auditors in a province | "High Auditor" |
| Chancellor | runs a provincial chancery | "Chancellor" |
| Archscribe | head of the Ledger | "Your Grace" |
The Ledger's soldiers are the **Vergers** — staff-and-mail temple guards in black and white.

### 4.3 Culture by region
- **Lowmarch (Ch.1).** Fens, reed-thatch, eel-traps, sheep on the higher ground, mud for nine months
  a year. Proud, poor, superstitious, funny. Reed-crosses hung over doors against Hollows.
  Food: eel pie, black bread, sloe gin. Folk songs about drowned lovers and clever poachers.
  Lowmarch people say "our" before a name for someone they love ("our Bren").
- **Crownlands.** Wheat and white stone. Courtly, formal, conscious of rank. Everyone is Named.
- **Westmarch.** Sea-cliffs, fishing, pirates-turned-merchants, a ducal court that thinks itself
  more civilised than the capital.
- **Thornwall.** Mountain holds, mining, goat-herding; hard people loyal to the border Margravine.
- **Skarnholt (the north, foreign).** Clan-holds in the snow. **No Ledger.** Oaths are sworn "on
  breath and iron" before the hearth; a broken oath is answered by the clan, so Hollows are rare and
  small. Runes. Considered barbarians in Aldmere; consider Aldmere a nation of clerks.
- **Velmont Concord (east, foreign).** Merchant republics across the Narrow Sea; contracts sworn
  through guild-houses that copy the Ledger's forms but answer to no church. Wealthy, cosmopolitan,
  still bitter about the Treaty of Seven Rivers.
- **The Scald (south, foreign/frontier).** Red badlands and salt flats beyond the Sallow's mouth;
  nomad caravans, old ruins from the Age of Broken Words, and **the Sarth** (lizardfolk) in the
  southern deltas, who keep oaths by carving them into living reeds.

### 4.4 Economy
- **Currency:** copper **pennies**, silver **marks** (20 pennies), gold **crowns** (12 marks).
  *(UI currency is currently labelled "gil" in the schema — see SCHEMA_REQUESTS.md.)*
- **The Tithe:** a tenth of income, sworn annually as a recorded "tithe-oath" by every Named
  household. The unrecorded pay a flat **hearth-penny** they cannot legally swear to, so it is
  collected by Notaries with Vergers.
- **Ledger fees:** Naming (2–5 marks), marriage (1 mark), contracts (a penny per line), Remissions
  (whatever the market will bear).
- **Free companies** (mercenaries) are hired by recorded contract. Hollow-escort work — guiding
  caravans through fen and blight with lanterns — is a Lowmarch speciality.
- **Kept oil** (Ledger-blessed lamp oil) is the second-largest Ledger income after tithes.

## 5. Factions and politics

### 5.1 The Regency (the Crown)
- **Head:** Lucan Valcourt, Lord Regent. Seat: the Palace of Aurenne and the fortress of
  **Corvel Keep** (Ashguard headquarters on the Lowmarch border).
- **Wants:** to find Aurelie (his oath), to hold the realm together until then, and to survive
  the Ledger, whom he suspects but cannot prove killed his brother.
- **Instruments:** **the Ashguard** — grey-cloaked soldiers founded to burn the blight; now the
  Regent's household army and most feared force in the realm. Commanded in the Lowmarch by
  **Captain Ilka Stroud**. Their creed: *"Ash today, harvest tomorrow."*
- **Perception:** tyrant, butcher, usurper-in-waiting. **Truth:** brutal, honest, desperate.

### 5.2 The Ledger
- **Head:** Archscribe Clement. Seat: **the Archive of Saint Verity**, a white mountain of
  libraries on an island in the Old Mere at Aurenne.
- **Wants (Clement):** a realm where every promise passes through his book — permanently. He
  believes that without the Ledger the Age of Broken Words returns, and he may be right.
- **Instruments:** Notaries (money), Vergers (force), **the Audit** (Auditors — the inquisition;
  black-sashed, grey-gloved), the Black Folio (power over the powerful).
- **Current plan:** Lucan is dying. Clement has spent twelve years raising the lost heir inside
  the Ledger as a devoted novice. When Lucan dies, the Ledger will "discover" her, crown her, and
  rule through her. Chapter 1 is the first move of that plan — and the first thing to go wrong.

### 5.3 The great houses
- **House Valcourt** (royal): Lucan; the missing Aurelie.
- **Duke Rowan Aubrey of the Westmarch** — charming, rich, ambitious; wants the Regency; a Ledger
  client (his father's broken marriage-oath was struck). Ch.2–4 antagonist/ally of convenience.
- **Margravine Isaura Thorne of the Thornwall** — old, blunt, guards the northern passes; distrusts
  everyone equally. Possible Ch.4 ally.
- **Count Benedek Sallis of the Lowmarch** — absentee lord of Tallow Cross, Tenpenny and Sallowmere,
  lives at court. His struck oaths are the debts remitted onto Tenpenny and Sallowmere. Ch.2
  antagonist; the man Tamsin's oath is really owed by.

### 5.4 The Unwritten
Not yet an organisation in Chapter 1 — a scatter of fen villages and outlaws swearing hedge oaths
in secret. Jory Tenpenny's people are among them. By Chapter 2 they are a movement; by Chapter 4 an
army. The Oathbound (Bren's company) become their sword and, reluctantly, their symbol.

### 5.5 Free companies
- **Brask's Lanterns** — Odo Brask's company: Hollow-escorts, small, honest-ish. Bren's home. Shattered
  in Ch.1; reborn as **the Oathbound**.
- **The Red Tithe** — a large, expensive company hired by Ledger Notaries to collect hearth-pennies.
  Generic enemies from Ch.2 on.
- **The Saltreach Corsairs** — Westmarch sea-raiders turned "privateers". Pack material.

### 5.6 Foreign powers
- **Skarnholt** (north): clan confederation under the Hearth-Lord **Hrodric Ashmantle** (Eira's
  father). Preparing to test the Thornwall passes while Aldmere is weak.
- **The Velmont Concord** (east): merchant republics; would love the Ashen Reach blamed on someone else.
- **The Scald** (south): no single power; caravans, nomads, Sarth deltas. Frontier.

### 5.7 Political map in one sentence per chapter
1. A box of records sets Regent, Ledger and the poor at each other's throats.
2. The Regent has the heir; the Ledger has the Regent's death in its pocket; the poor have an army.
3. The Ledger crowns a queen it owns; the Regent's soldiers become rebels.
4. Foreign war and blight; the queen signs what she must; friends become enemies.
5. The record itself is the battlefield.

## 6. Geography (narrative)

**Aldmere** is a broad green kingdom between mountains and sea. Its heart is **the Old Mere**, the
great lake on whose island the Archive stands, with the white capital **Aurenne** on the shore.
The river **Aure** runs south from the Thornwall through Aurenne and the Old Mere into the
Lowmarch, where it joins the slow brown **Sallow**, which winds south-west through fen and farmland
to the Grey Sea at **Saltmouth**.

- **North:** the **Thornwall Mountains**; beyond them the snows of **Skarnholt**.
- **West:** the **Westmarch** — cliffs, forests and the port of **Saltreach**; the **Grey Sea**.
- **Centre:** the **Crownlands** — wheat, white towns, the Old Mere, Aurenne.
- **East:** the **Ashen Reach** — the blight of 594 AL, behind the Ashguard's burn-line; then the
  **Narrow Sea** and the **Velmont Concord**.
- **South:** the **Lowmarch** (Chapter 1) — farmland and the great marsh of the **Greyfen**; south of
  it the coast, and beyond the Sallow's mouth the red badlands of **the Scald**.

### Chapter 1 places (the Lowmarch)

## 6A. World geography for the generator

### Coordinate convention
- The hand-authored kingdom is described in **kingdom map units (KMU) 0..1023** on each axis,
  **y = 0 north**. **1 KMU = 4 world tiles**, so the authored kingdom is ~**4096 × 4096 world
  tiles**. World tiles are battle-scale tiles (a battle carves a ~15×15 patch of world tiles).
- The overworld is **endless and procedurally generated per chunk** (32-bit tile coordinates). The
  kingdom sits at its centre. Everything **outside the box 0..1023 KMU is the Frontier** — unexplored
  lands for future campaign packs and online play (see §10). The generator should continue the
  biome of each border region outward and blend into frontier biomes: snow/tundra north of
  Skarnholt, sea west of the Grey Sea coast (with islands), the Velmont coast and isles east,
  desert and badlands south of the Scald.
- `world.ts` node `pos` values are in KMU. Edges are **roads** for the generator to draw (cobble
  `r` within towns and on the King's Road, dirt `d` elsewhere, causeways of `d`/`o` across marsh).
- **Danger** 1–10 sets roaming-party level: suggested roaming level ≈ `danger × 2 ± 1`, capped by
  story progress in story mode.

## 8. The saga: five chapters and an epilogue

> Working titles. Each chapter is a full campaign arc of ~6–8 story battles plus skirmishes, ending
> on a turn. Each new chapter shifts the region of the world map.

### Chapter 1 — *The Split Penny* (the Lowmarch) — **written; see chapters/chapter1.md**
Brask's Lanterns take a Ledger contract to carry a locked box of records and a novice — Hesper —
from Millbrook to the capital. Regent's Ashguard and Ledger Auditors both want the box. The Auditor
Morrow betrays the company; Odo dies at the Sallow Ford; Bren takes up the lantern. Fugitives in the
Greyfen, the party gathers a fen poacher, a Struck bandit, an oathbreaker knight and a northern
scholar, learns that hedge oaths are real and becomes **the Oathbound**. On Hollin Tor they defeat
Morrow; Hesper opens the box and reads her own name — **Aurelie Valcourt**. The Regent arrives,
kneels to his niece, kills Morrow. Hesper swears to go with Lucan if he lets the Oathbound live —
and asks Bren to witness it. **Sting:** Archscribe Clement: *"A man with no name cannot be struck.
Find him one."*

### Chapter 2 — *The Unwritten* (Lowmarch → Crownlands; Corvel Keep, Aurenne)
- **Bren's line:** outlaws in the fens; the Oathbound ally with the Unwritten. A raid on Count
  Sallis's tithe-barns uncovers a Notary's ledger of Strikings: the **Remittance** — Sallowmere's and
  Tenpenny's Hollows were Sallis's struck debts. Tamsin's oath turns toward the Count. Eira decodes
  the Ledger's cipher. Aldous leaves to go to Aurelie's side.
- **Hesper's line (interludes):** at Corvel Keep and Aurenne, Lucan teaches her the realm. She sees
  the Ashguard's ugly necessity. She learns Lucan is dying because his oath can't be kept while her
  Naming is struck. The Ledger offers to **re-Name** her publicly at the Market of Vows.
- **Midpoint:** Bren sneaks into Aurenne; rooftop reunion; the old banter; the new distance.
- **Turn:** at the Rite of Re-Naming, Lucan's oath is fulfilled — he straightens, colour in his face —
  and in that moment the Ledger strikes his own Naming (Clement, from the Archive). He dies in Hesper's
  arms. An Auditor's crossbow bolt "proves" an unrecorded assassin: **Bren is framed.**
- **End:** Hesper, in public, must condemn Bren. In private, she sends Aldous to warn him.

### Chapter 3 — *The Crowned Lie* (Crownlands, Westmarch)
- Hesper is crowned **Queen Aurelie** by Clement. Duke Aubrey of the Westmarch revolts. Bren is the
  most wanted man in Aldmere.
- **Ilka Stroud**, certain the Ledger murdered Lucan, defects with a third of the Ashguard. Tamsin
  and Ilka — hatred under one roof.
- Aldous reveals what he saw the Night of the Striking: Clement's Auditors in the palace before the
  king died; the order to "secure the girl".
- The Oathbound steal a page of the Black Folio from the Saltreach chancery: proof that Edric's Crown
  Oath was struck.
- **Turn:** Hesper confronts Clement in the Archive. He shows her her own Naming entry, re-written
  in his book, his seal beside it: **he can strike the queen.** She is a hostage on her own throne.
  She gives the stolen page back to save Bren's company from a trap — and Bren sees only that she
  returned it.

### Chapter 4 — *Ledger of Ash* (Thornwall, Ashen Reach)
- Skarnholt crosses the Thornwall (Eira's letters timed it). The blight surges west — the Great
  Hollow, the pooled debt of 180 years of Remittance, is waking in the Ashen Reach.
- Under Clement's threat, Queen Aurelie signs the **Edict of Ashing** (burn the eastern Lowmarch to
  slow the blight) and outlaws hedge oaths.
- Eira breaks her clan oath to stay with the Oathbound. Ilka saves Tamsin and kneels.
- Clement has been tracking Bren through the one recorded thing that touches him: Hesper's half of
  their oath. **Hesper strikes their childhood oath** with her royal seal to blind him — not knowing
  that its weight will be remitted onto the nearest unrecorded soul: Bren. He begins to Hollow.
- Aldous dies saving Hesper from an Auditor's assassination; the Grey Dog lies down beside him.
- **End:** Bren, greying, and Hesper, crowned, face each other across the burn-line at Corvel Keep.
  "You struck me out." "I struck you *free*." Both are right.

### Chapter 5 — *The Unbroken Word* (Aurenne, the Archive, the Old Mere)
- Clement binds the Black Folio to the Great Hollow, meaning to "audit" the realm: every unpaid debt
  in the book will come due at once — on the unrecorded.
- The Unwritten, the Ashguard rebels, the Thornwall and even Skarn clans converge on Aurenne.
- Hesper, before the whole city in the Market of Vows, re-swears the rooftop oath with Bren — a hedge
  oath witnessed by thousands. The debt on Bren is lifted: the oath is kept again.
- Final battles: the Lakegate, the Archive bridges, the vault of the Black Folio; Clement fused with
  the Great Hollow.
- **The choice (canon):** burn the Ledger. Every recorded oath in Aldmere — every marriage, debt,
  fealty, treaty, Remission and Remittance — is dissolved. The Great Hollow loses its debts and
  dies. The realm wakes up free and terrified.

### Epilogue — *Witness*
Queen Aurelie (who signs her letters "Hesper") rules by an oath sworn in the open and witnessed by
the crowd; she must rebuild a kingdom where people choose again which promises to make. Bren refuses
a title, keeps no name but Holloway, and walks the roads with the Oathbound as a hedge witness. Last
scene: a crossroads at dusk; two children ask a tired man with a lantern to witness a promise. He
smiles. *"I witness you."* — *"And I you."*

## 11. Dialogue style guide

### 11.1 Format rules (talking-head boxes)
- **Max ~180 characters per line.** Prefer 60–140. Break speeches into consecutive lines at natural
  breaths; each line should be speakable in one breath by the actor.
- One thought per line. Let the other character interrupt — conversations, not monologues.
- Use `direction` on every emotionally loaded line: what the character *means*, not just the tone
  ("lying, badly", "trying not to cry, deflecting with a joke"). Directions are for actors; keep them
  short and active.
- Use `style: 'shout'` sparingly (battle cries, real anger); `'whisper'` for secrets and the
  dying; `'thought'` for inner voice (rare; Bren and Hesper only).
- **Expressions** (neutral, happy, angry, sad, surprised, determined, hurt, smirk) are the portrait,
  not the voice. A character can say a sad line with a `smirk` portrait if they're deflecting.
- **VO ids** are permanent: `main.c<chapter>.s<NN>.<NNN>`. Never renumber. Insert with suffixes
  (`014a`). Battle-trigger scenes use s40–s99 within a chapter (see chapter1.md).

### 11.2 Language
- **No modern slang or idiom:** no "okay", "yeah", "guys", "cool", "deal with it", "for real",
  "seconds/minutes" (use "a moment", "a breath", "an hour"), "kids" (use "children", "bairns" for
  Lowmarch only), "boss" (use "captain", "chief"). No technology metaphors.
- **Mild, in-world profanity only:** "Hollow take it", "ink and ashes", "Witness blind me",
  "drowned dog", "bloody" (sparingly). No real-world religious oaths (no "God", "hell", "damn").
- **Reading level:** roughly ages 12+; vocabulary rich but plain. A difficult word must earn its
  place and be clear from context.
- **Contractions:** most characters use them. Morrow and Eira don't. Hesper avoids them until moved.
- **Exposition:** never two characters telling each other what they both know. Exposition comes
  from conflict (someone wants something), questions from outsiders (Tamsin, Eira), or jokes.
- **Lore words** used in dialogue: Ledger, Naming, the Witness, Hollow, struck, unrecorded, hedge
  oath, surety, witnesslight, Remittance (only from C2), the Black Folio (only from C3).

## 12. Authoring workflow (adding chapters and packs)

1. **Plan** in this bible first: update §8 (saga), §9 (twists/seeds) and any character arcs you touch.
2. **Beat sheet:** `docs/story/chapters/chapterN.md` — battles (location, objective, why it matters, roster,
   triggers, map concept), scenes, party flow, levels, VO id ranges.
3. **Script:** write dialogue in the screenplay format in `docs/story/script/` (see `act1.txt` for the
   grammar: `== c1.sNN | Title | loc= | map= | music=`, `@ actor x y dir; …`, `speaker{Name}.expression!: text || direction`
   with `!` shout, `~` whisper, `^` thought; `NARR:`; `[stage …]`; `CHOICE`/`- option => flag:x goto:y`; `[label]`/`[goto]`).
   Run `python3 docs/story/script/compile.py` — it validates speakers, expressions, line length (≤180),
   locations, staging tiles and labels, assigns VO ids in order, writes `chapter1/scenes/act*.ts` and the
   voice script `chapters/chapter1_vo_script.md`. (For a new chapter, copy the compiler and change the
   `c1` prefix / output paths.) **Once a chapter has been recorded, never re-order lines** — append with
   suffixes (`014a`) instead, because the compiler numbers lines sequentially.
4. **Data:** characters/enemies/locations/world/maps/battles/chapter index under
   `src/content/campaigns/<pack>/`. Run `npx tsc --noEmit` and `npx vitest run tests/campaign-main.test.ts`
   (copy the test for a new pack).
5. **Engine asks** go in `STORY_JOBS.md` and `SCHEMA_REQUESTS.md` — never edit `schema.ts` from the story side.
6. **Campaign packs** get their own folder, id prefix (`pack.c1.s01.001`), cast entries here (§7 / new section),
   a region in the Frontier (§6A) and a pitch entry in §10.

## 13. Glossary
- **Archive** — the Ledger's seat on Archive Isle, Aurenne.
- **Ashguard** — the Regent's grey-cloaked soldiers; "to ash" = to burn a Hollow-struck place.
- **Auditor** — Ledger inquisitor. **The Audit** — the inquisition.
- **Black Folio** — secret register of every struck entry.
- **Hedge oath** — an oath sworn without the Ledger.
- **Hollow** — the shade of a broken oath's debt. **Restless** — a Hollow wearing a corpse.
  **Gaunt** — a noble-faced Hollow that feeds on grace. **Titheling** — small, grasping Hollow.
- **Kept oil** — Ledger-blessed lamp oil; burns with witnesslight.
- **Lanternbearer** — the old hedge-witness tradition; Odo, then Bren.
- **Naming** — recorded birth-oath. **Unrecorded** — people without one.
- **Oathbound** — Bren's company, sworn to each other with hedge oaths.
- **Remittance** — [secret] struck debts falling on the unrecorded.
- **Striking** — [secret in its full meaning] removing an entry from the Ledger.
- **Struck, the** — Lowmarch slang for villages plagued by Hollows they didn't earn (the people
  think it means "struck by misfortune"; the real meaning is literal).
- **Surety** — an oath's weight. **Grace** — the strength a kept oath gives.
- **Vergers** — Ledger temple guards.
- **Witnesslight** — fire near a kept oath; Hollows hate it.
